Sentence 1 says what that one thing is.

Good, useful, important, interesting — these say nothing. Name the real quality instead.

Add a comparison

A comparison forces you to take a side. Three ways to do it:

  • …better than a class can.
  • …more than any other subject.
  • …in a way that homework cannot.

This is the fastest way to turn a flat sentence into an arguable one.

Evidence is something you can count or picture

  • A number. six hours a week · forty minutes · two shifts
  • A person in a situation. a student who works Saturdays at a coffee shop
  • A cost or a trade-off. what a phone costs in hours instead of dollars
  • A comparison with the alternative. something a class on budgeting never makes real

The introduction has three moves

Start wide, finish narrow. Three or four sentences, about 50 words.

Move 1 — the hook

One sentence that makes the reader want the next one. Five kinds work:

SceneEvery Saturday morning, thousands of teenagers put on a uniform and go to work at a coffee shop or a store.
QuestionHow old should someone be before they earn their first paycheck?
NumberBy eleven in the morning, a student who skipped breakfast has already been awake for five hours.
Short storyMy cousin bought her first laptop with money she earned washing dishes.
QuotationAs an old teaching proverb puts it, “involve me and I learn.”
Two of these have a conditionA number and a quotation are only allowed if you can say where they came from. If you cannot, choose one of the other three.

The scene is the safest choice: it needs no research and no clever phrasing.

Move 2 — the background

One or two sentences that give the reader the disagreement. This is what makes an essay a discussion instead of a speech.

Some parents worry that a job takes time away from schoolwork. A part-time job, however, teaches things a classroom cannot.

Sentence 1 gives the other side. Sentence 2 turns toward yours.

Your turn

Which sentence is the background move?

Move 3 — the thesis

The most important sentence in the essay. One formula covers it:

The formula[your position] + because + [reason 1], [reason 2], and [reason 3].

High school students should be encouraged to work a few hours a week, because a job builds financial awareness, develops responsibility, and improves their chances of getting hired later.

Two things to copy: the three reasons are in the order your paragraphs will use them, and they have the same grammar — builds… develops… improves…

Your turn

What is wrong with this thesis? Students should work part-time because it teaches money skills, and there is time management, and being patient.

The conclusion has three moves too

  • Say your thesis again — in new words. Not one phrase copied.
  • Answer “so what?” Who should do what now?
  • Add nothing new. No new argument, no new example, no new number.

In short, a few working hours each week give students practical skill with money, a stronger sense of responsibility, and a real advantage when they look for their first full-time job. Schools and parents should treat a part-time job not as a distraction from education but as a part of it.

Say it again in new words

Move 1 of the conclusion is the hardest sentence in the essay: the same idea, none of the same phrases. Three ways to do it.

Swap the wordsbuilds financial awareness → gives students practical skill with money
Turn the verb into a nounstudents learn responsibility → a stronger sense of responsibility
Flip it arounda job improves their chances of being hired → employers notice a student who has worked
In conclusion, students should be encouraged to work a few hours a week because it builds financial awareness.Copied. Four phrases straight from the thesis.
In short, a few working hours each week give students practical skill with money.Same claim, new words. Nothing is copied.

Give the other side one paragraph of respect

Somewhere in body 3, admit what the other side gets right — then answer it.

Which sentence is a concession?

Joining two sentences

Signposts like however and as a result are not glue. A comma on its own cannot hold two sentences together. That mistake is called a comma splice, and it is the one teachers see most.

A job takes time, however it teaches things school cannot.Two complete sentences joined by a comma. The comma is not strong enough.
A job takes time. However, it teaches things school cannot.Period, then the signpost, then a comma after it.
A job takes time, but it teaches things school cannot.But, and, so are the only joining words a comma can work with.
The quick testCover the comma. Is each half a complete sentence on its own? Then you need a period, a semicolon, or but / and / so.

Read one finished essay

Everything you have practiced, in 346 words. The colors are the sentence jobs you already know.

HookBackgroundThesisPointEvidenceExplanationLink

Every Saturday morning, thousands of teenagers put on a uniform and go to work at a coffee shop or a store. Some parents worry that this takes time away from schoolwork. A part-time job, however, teaches lessons that a classroom cannot. High school students should be encouraged to work a few hours a week, because a job builds financial awareness, develops responsibility, and improves their chances of getting hired later.

First of all, a part-time job teaches students to manage money better than any class can. A student who works six hours a week quickly learns what a new phone really costs — not in dollars, but in hours behind a counter. This lesson lands harder than any classroom exercise, because the money is real and the decision is the student’s own. In other words, a part-time job turns an abstract idea into a daily habit.

Second, a job builds a sense of responsibility that school finds hard to teach. At work, a teenager has to show up on time, take instructions from an adult who is not a parent or a teacher, and stay polite with rude customers. As a result, they learn that their actions have real consequences for other people. That new maturity often shows up in their behavior at school as well.

Most importantly, a first job improves a student’s chances of being hired later. Employers repeatedly say they value applicants who can prove they have worked on a team, and a first job is that proof. A student who has spent a year behind a counter starts ahead of a classmate whose résumé is still empty. Admittedly, some students work too many hours, but the answer to that is a limit, not a ban. Used sensibly, those few hours pay a student back for years.

In short, a few working hours each week give students practical skill with money, a stronger sense of responsibility, and a real advantage when they look for their first full-time job. Schools and parents should treat a part-time job not as a distraction from education but as a part of it.
