# Standards alignment for the five proficiency bands

Prepared 2026-09-05 for the ESL grammar-and-writing platform. Bands: **Band 1 = CEFR A1, Band 2 = A2, Band 3 = B1, Band 4 = B2, Band 5 = C1.**

How to read this file
- Every bullet in Section B carries a tag naming the standard it comes from (tag key at the top of Section B). Short quotations are verbatim from the cited document; anything that is my own judgement is labelled **inference**.
- Grade-band anchors (Texas TEKS, Virginia SOL) describe what native-speaker classrooms expect at an age; ELD frameworks (CA ELD, TX ELPS, WIDA) describe proficiency regardless of age. The two are combined here only for *conventions and sentence complexity*, never for topics or reading level (inference, see D1).
- Method caveat: several official PDFs (CA ELD Appendix B, WIDA framework, TX PLDs, VA 2024 K-12 SOL) could only be read through a text-extraction proxy; where a passage could not be recovered verbatim I say so and cite a secondary reproduction. The VA 2024 K-12 document was readable only through grade 5, so grades 8, 9 and 11 come from district documents that reproduce the 2024 codes verbatim (Section E).

## A. Master table

| Band | CEFR | WIDA level(s) (2020 framework, 6 levels) | California ELD (2012) level | Texas ELPS (19 TAC §74.4, current) → revised ELPS 2026-27 | Virginia SOL grade band (2017 numbering / 2024 codes) | The writer at this band (one line) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A1 | 1 Entering → early 2 Emerging | Emerging (entry to mid) | Beginning → Pre-Production / Beginning | Grades 1-2 (1.13, 2.11 / 2.LU) | Labels, lists, copies and writes short formulaic sentences on very familiar topics, mostly present tense; meaning often depends on the reader's goodwill. |
| 2 | A2 | 2 Emerging → 3 Developing | Emerging (exit) → early Expanding | Intermediate → Intermediate | Grades 3-5 (3.9, 4.8, 5.8 / 3.LU, 5.LU) | Writes a series of short simple and compound sentences joined with *and/but/because* into a short paragraph on a concrete, familiar topic; present tense reliable, past/future attempted but inconsistent. |
| 3 | B1 | 3 Developing → 4 Expanding | Expanding | Advanced → High Intermediate | Grades 6-8 (6.8, 7.8, 8.8 / 8.LU) | Writes straightforward connected multi-paragraph texts (narrate, inform, short simple essay) using complex sentences, consistent tense and common cohesive devices; errors noticeable but meaning clear. |
| 4 | B2 | 4 Expanding → 5 Bridging | Bridging | Advanced High → Advanced | Grades 9-10 (9.7, 10.7 / 9.LU) | Writes clear, detailed essays that develop an argument and synthesise sources, with controlled variety (parallel structure, appositives, compound-complex sentences, passive voice); good grammatical control; cites in MLA/APA. |
| 5 | C1 | 5 Bridging → 6 Reaching (grades 9-12 cluster only) | Bridging exit / reclassified (beyond the ELD standards' scope) | beyond Advanced High (post-exit; TEKS English III-IV) | Grades 11-12 (11.7, 12.7 / 11.LU) | Writes well-structured texts on complex subjects with nominalisation, verbals, embedded clauses and register control; errors rare; produces a full research product to a style manual. |

Anchoring evidence for the rows (details and quotations in Sections B and D):
- Band 1-2 vs Texas: Beginning writing is "label, list, and copy" with "present tense used primarily"; Intermediate is "short, simple sentences" with "present tense used most accurately; simple future and past tenses ... inconsistently" [TX ELPS (d)(6)(A)(iii), (B)(iii)(V)] — this matches CEFR A1 "simple isolated phrases and sentences" and A2 "series of simple phrases and sentences linked with simple connectors" [CEFR-WP].
- Band 2-3-4 vs California: the clause-combining strand moves from "compound sentences using and, but, so; ... complex sentences using because" (Emerging) to "compound and complex sentences" that "express a reason ... make a concession ... or ... link two ideas that happen at the same time" (Bridging, grade 6 wording) to "compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences" (Bridging, grade 7) [CA PII.6.6.Em, PII.6.6.Br, PII.7.6.Br] — the same ladder CEFR uses from A2 connectors to B2 "cohesive devices" [CEFR-CO]. The Expanding text sits between these two (inference; not retrieved verbatim).
- WIDA: WIDA itself warns that its proficiency levels should not be equated with CEFR levels (only MODEL Online *scale scores* were linked) [WIDA-CEFR]; the one published level-to-CEFR alignment (Pearson GSE, grades 6-8) gives overlapping ranges: Level 2 = A1-B1, Level 3 = A2+-B1+, Level 4 = A2+-B2+, Level 5 = B1-B2+, Level 6 = B1-B2+ [WIDA-GSE]. Hence each band spans two adjacent WIDA levels (inference).
- Band 5: WIDA MODEL measures up to C1 only in grades 9-12 (B2/C1 in 6-8) [WIDA-CEFR]; CA ELD Bridging and TX Advanced High both describe writing "nearly comparable to" native peers, i.e. the exit point of ELD support, so C1 is mapped to mainstream grade 11-12 expectations (inference, see D3).

## B. Band-by-band grammar and writing expectations

Tag key
- **CEFR-GS** global scale; **CEFR-WP** Overall written production; **CEFR-RE** Reports and essays; **CEFR-CO** Coherence and cohesion; **CEFR-GA** Grammatical accuracy; **CEFR-VR** Vocabulary range; **CEFR-OC** Orthographic control (Council of Europe, 2001 scales / 2020 Companion Volume).
- **CI-A1 … CI-C1** grammar inventory for that level in the British Council–EAQUALS *Core Inventory for General English* (2nd ed. 2015).
- **WIDA-PD Ln** 2012 Performance Definitions, expressive, level n; **WIDA-LC 6-8 Ln / 9-12 Ln** WIDA Language Charts (aligned to the 2020 Proficiency Level Descriptors and revised ACCESS), expressive; **WIDA-LE** 2020 Language Expectations (ELD-2, grades 6-8); **WIDA-KLU** Key Language Uses.
- **CA PII.g.n.Em/Ex/Br** California ELD Standards 2012, Part II standard n for grade g at Emerging/Expanding/Bridging; **CA App-B** Appendix B of the same document; **ELPAC-PLD** Summative ELPAC General Performance Level Descriptors.
- **TX ELPS (c)(5)** cross-curricular writing expectations; **TX ELPS (d)(6)(A-D)** writing PLDs grades 2-12; **TX ELPS-26 Ln** revised ELPS (SBOE 2024, effective 2026-27) grades 4-12 PLDs, level n (1 Pre-Production, 2 Beginning, 3 Intermediate, 4 High Intermediate, 5 Advanced); **TEKS g.s.D.i** TEKS ELAR 2017 (e.g., TEKS 3.11.D.ii; E1 = English I).
- **VA17 g.s.x** Virginia 2017 English SOL; **VA24 g.LU.n** Virginia 2024 English SOL, Language Usage strand (LU.1 Grammar, LU.2 Mechanics), **VA24 g.W.3**, **VA24 g.R.1**.

### Band 1 — CEFR A1 (WIDA 1-2 · CA Emerging · TX Beginning · VA grades 1-2)

Writer profile
- "Can write simple isolated phrases and sentences" [CEFR-WP A1]; "limited control of a few simple grammatical structures and sentence patterns in a learnt repertoire" [CEFR-GA A1]; links ideas "with very basic linear connectors like 'and' or 'then'" [CEFR-CO A1].
- Texas Beginning: "ability to label, list, and copy"; "high-frequency words/phrases and short, simple sentences ... based primarily on recently practiced, memorized, or highly familiar material"; "present tense used primarily"; errors "may significantly hinder or prevent understanding" [TX ELPS (d)(6)(A)(iii)]. Revised ELPS: Beginning "write using simple phrases or patterns that may convey ideas"; ELA "write simple phrases using present tense or present progressive tense verbs" [TX ELPS-26 L2].
- WIDA: "Words, phrases, or chunks of language" / "Phrase-level grammatical structures" (L1); "Phrases or short sentences" / "Formulaic grammatical structures" (L2) [WIDA-PD L1-L2]; "simple sentences with emerging variation in sentence structure", ideas ordered "using sequencing, repetition, and transitions" [WIDA-LC 6-8 L1].
- California Emerging (kindergarten wording, the base of the Part II ladder): "Use frequently used verbs (e.g., go, eat, run)" and "simple verb tenses ... (e.g., simple past for recounting an experience)" [CA PII.K.3a.Em, PII.K.3b.Em]; ELPAC Level 1 "corresponds to the 'Emerging' proficiency level" [ELPAC-PLD].

Grammar to control
- Sentence = subject + verb; "complete sentences with subject-verb agreement" [TEKS 2.11.D.i; VA17 1.13.a]; statements, questions, commands with matching end punctuation [VA24 2.LU.2; VA17 2.11.b].
- *be* (affirmative, negative, question), *there is/are*, present simple, present continuous, imperatives (+/−) [CI-A1]; present/present-progressive is the only reliable tense [TX ELPS (d)(6)(A)(iii); TX ELPS-26 L2].
- Introduce past simple of *be* and regular past simple for recounting [CI-A1; CA PII.K.3b.Em]; TEKS expects "past, present, and future verb tense" already in grade 2 [TEKS 2.11.D.ii] — treat future *going to* as receptive at this band (inference; see D5).
- Nouns: singular/plural, common/proper; regular and "frequently occurring irregular plural nouns (e.g., men, teeth)" [TEKS 2.11.D.iii; VA24 2.LU.1]; possessive *'s* [CI-A1; VA17 2.11.f].
- Articles *a/an/the* as part of "adjectives, including articles" [TEKS 2.11.D.iv]; *how much/how many* with very common uncountables [CI-A1].
- Pronouns: "subjective, objective, and possessive cases" [TEKS 2.11.D.vii]; possessive adjectives, simple personal pronouns [CI-A1].
- Adjectives: common and demonstrative; very basic intensifiers (*very*); first comparatives/superlatives of short adjectives [CI-A1]; "use adjectives correctly" [VA17 2.11.k].
- Adverbs of frequency; adverbs of time and place [CI-A1; TEKS 2.11.D.v].
- Prepositions of place and time (*in/on/at*), common prepositional phrases [CI-A1; TEKS 2.11.D.vi].
- Conjunctions: *and, but, or, so, because* "to signal simple relationships" [VA24 2.LU.1]; coordinating conjunctions "to form compound subjects and predicates" [TEKS 2.11.D.viii].
- Modals: *can/can't, could/couldn't*; *I'd like*; *like/love/hate + -ing* [CI-A1].
- Questions: yes/no and wh-questions [CI-A1]; answering "yes-no and wh- questions ... using simple phrases" [CA PI.7.1.Em].
- Mechanics: capital letter to start a sentence and for *I*, proper nouns, months/days [VA17 2.11.c; TEKS 2.11.D.ix]; end punctuation, apostrophes in contractions, commas in a series and in dates [TEKS 2.11.D.x]; spelling of "commonly used sight words and phonetically regular words" [VA17 1.13.c]; A1 orthography = "Can copy familiar words and short phrases" [CEFR-OC A1].

Writing expectations
- Labels, lists, captions, copied models, then single sentences and 3-5 sentence "short paragraphs" on memorised material [TX ELPS (d)(6)(A)(iii)].
- Sentence frames and formulaic chunks ("Formulaic grammatical structures") are the norm [WIDA-PD L2].
- No essay or research expectation; TEKS grade 2 only asks pupils to "cite sources appropriately" orally/simply (inference from TEKS 4.13.G vertical alignment note).

Vocabulary
- "basic vocabulary repertoire of words/signs and phrases related to particular concrete situations" [CEFR-VR A1]; "Everyday social and instructional words and expressions" [WIDA-PD L1]; "high-frequency words/phrases" [TX ELPS (d)(6)(A)(iii)].

### Band 2 — CEFR A2 (WIDA 2-3 · CA Emerging-exit/early Expanding · TX Intermediate · VA grades 3-5)

Writer profile
- "Can write a series of simple phrases and sentences linked with simple connectors like 'and', 'but' and 'because'" [CEFR-WP A2]; "Uses some simple structures correctly, but still systematically makes basic mistakes; nevertheless, it is usually clear what they are trying to say" [CEFR-GA A2].
- Texas Intermediate: "simple, original messages consisting of short, simple sentences; frequent inaccuracies occur when creating or taking risks beyond familiar English"; "high-frequency vocabulary; academic writing often has an oral tone"; "loosely connected text with limited use of cohesive devices or repetitive use"; "present tense used most accurately; simple future and past tenses, if attempted, are used inconsistently or with frequent inaccuracies"; "undetailed descriptions, explanations, and narrations" [TX ELPS (d)(6)(B)(iii)(I-VI)]. Revised ELPS Intermediate: "write sentences using past, present, perfect, or progressive tense verbs"; "write phrases using standard conventions" [TX ELPS-26 L3].
- WIDA: "Short and some expanded sentences with emerging complexity" / "Simple and compound grammatical structures with occasional variation" (L3) [WIDA-PD L3]; "simple sentences, occasionally adding dependent clauses" and cohesion "with demonstratives, pronouns, and conjunctions" (L2), then "simple and compound sentences, showing initial attempts at sentence variation" (L3) [WIDA-LC 6-8 L2-L3].
- California: Emerging clause-combining = "creating compound sentences using and, but, so" [CA PII.6.6.Em] and "creating complex sentences using because" [CA PII.9-10.6.Em]; cohesion = "how pronouns refer back to nouns in text" [CA PII.6.2a.Em]; condensing "by compounding verbs, adding prepositional phrases, or through simple embedded clauses" [CA PII.6.7.Em].

Grammar to control
- Simple **and compound** sentences "with subject-verb agreement and avoidance of splices, run-ons, and fragments" [TEKS 3.11.D.i, 5.11.D.i]; "Produce, expand, and rearrange simple and compound sentences" [VA24 3.LU.1, 5.LU.1]; "Distinguish between complete and incomplete sentences" [VA24 3.LU.1]; edit "for fragments and run-on sentences" [VA17 5.8].
- Tenses: past simple incl. "past tense of irregular verbs" [TEKS 4/5.11.D.ii; VA24 3.LU.1], past continuous, future with *will* and *going to*, present continuous for future, first present perfect [CI-A2]; past/present/future required [TEKS 3.11.D.ii]; CA Expanding adds the perfect to the list of tenses "appropriate to the task, text type, and discipline" (grade 6 wording, verified for the Bridging text) [CA PII.6.3.Br].
- Verb patterns: gerunds; *like/want/would like* + -ing/infinitive [CI-A2].
- Modals: *can/could, have to, should* [CI-A2].
- Conditionals: zero and first [CI-A2].
- Nouns: countable/uncountable with *much/many*, articles with count/uncount nouns [CI-A2]; collective nouns [TEKS 5.11.D.iii]; plural possessives [VA17 5.8]; possessives *'s / s'* [CI-A2].
- Pronouns: indefinite pronouns [TEKS 5.11.D.vii; VA24 2.LU.1]; "noun-pronoun agreement" [VA17 4.8.c; VA24 5.LU.1].
- Adjectives/adverbs: comparatives with *than*, superlatives with *the* [CI-A2]; "comparative and superlative forms" [TEKS 3.11.D.iv, 5.11.D.iv; VA24 3.LU.1, 5.LU.1]; "adjective and adverb comparisons" [VA17 5.8]; adverbs of time and manner [TEKS 3.11.D.v]; adverbial phrases of time, place, frequency incl. word order [CI-A2]; "Eliminate double negatives" [VA17 4.8.b, 5.8].
- Prepositional phrases of place, time and movement [CI-A2; TEKS 3.11.D.vi; VA17 5.8]; CA Emerging "Expand sentences with simple adverbials (e.g., adverbs, adverb phrases, prepositional phrases) to provide details (e.g., time, manner, place, cause)" [CA PII.6.5.Em].
- Noun phrases: "Expand noun phrases in simple ways (e.g., adding a sensory adjective to a noun)" [CA PII.6.4.Em].
- Conjunctions: coordinating for compound sentences [TEKS 3.11.D.viii; VA17 5.8]; first subordinating conjunctions "to form complex sentences" and conjunctive adverbs [TEKS 5.11.D.viii, v]; "coordinating and subordinating conjunctions" [VA24 5.LU.1].
- Questions: wh-questions in the past; common phrasal verbs [CI-A2].
- Mechanics: commas "in series, dates, addresses, and letters"; "commas and quotation marks to indicate dialogue"; apostrophes for contractions and possession [VA24 5.LU.2; TEKS 5.11.D.x]; capitalisation of titles, holidays, geographical names, abbreviations/acronyms [TEKS 3.11.D.ix, 5.11.D.ix; VA24 3.LU.2]; spelling with "grade-appropriate orthographic patterns and rules and high-frequency words" [TEKS 5.11.D.xi], "common homophones" [VA17 4.8.g].

Writing expectations
- A short paragraph (5-8 sentences) that narrates, describes or explains one idea; multiple related ideas only just emerging ("expanded expression of one idea or emerging expression of multiple related ideas") [WIDA-PD L3]; "undetailed descriptions, explanations, and narrations" [TX ELPS (d)(6)(B)(iii)(VI)].
- Text-level cohesion with "everyday connecting words" such as *first/next* (CA grade 5 Emerging example) [CA App-B]; pronoun reference back to nouns [CA PII.6.2a.Em].
- Source use is procedural only: "create a works cited page" (grade 3) / "develop a bibliography" (grades 4-5) [TEKS 3.13.G, 4.13.G, 5.13.G]; "Avoid plagiarism and give proper credit by providing citations" [VA24 5.R.1]. No formal style (inference).

Vocabulary
- "sufficient vocabulary to conduct routine everyday transactions involving familiar situations and topics" [CEFR-VR A2]; "Social and instructional words and expressions across content areas" (L2), "Words or expressions with multiple meanings" (L3) [WIDA-PD L2-L3]; "high-frequency vocabulary" with "repetition of ideas due to lack of vocabulary" [TX ELPS (d)(6)(B)(iii)(II, IV)].

### Band 3 — CEFR B1 (WIDA 3-4 · CA Expanding · TX Advanced · VA grades 6-8)

Writer profile
- "Can write straightforward connected texts on a range of familiar subjects ... by linking a series of shorter discrete elements into a linear sequence" [CEFR-WP B1]; "Can write short, simple essays on topics of interest" [CEFR-RE B1]; "reasonable accuracy in familiar contexts; generally good control, though with noticeable mother-tongue influence" [CEFR-GA B1].
- Texas Advanced: "grasp of basic verbs, tenses, grammar features, and sentence patterns; partial grasp of more complex verbs, tenses, grammar features, and sentence patterns"; "emerging grade-appropriate vocabulary; academic writing has a more academic tone"; "use of a variety of common cohesive devices, although some redundancy may occur"; "narrations, explanations, and descriptions developed in some detail with emerging clarity" [TX ELPS (d)(6)(C)(iii)(I-IV)]. Revised ELPS High Intermediate: "write sentences using transition words that convey ideas with increasing accuracy"; "past, present, perfect, or progressive tense verbs with subject-verb agreement with increasing accuracy" [TX ELPS-26 L4].
- WIDA: "Short, expanded, and some complex sentences" / "Compound and complex grammatical structures" (L4) [WIDA-PD L4]; cohesion "using synonyms and parallel expressions" (L3) then "compound and complex sentences, refining control over different sentence structures" and "abstract nouns and adverbials to add clarity about when, how, and where" (L4) [WIDA-LC 6-8 L3-L4]. Language Arts expectations at this cluster: Narrate = "Orient audience to context and point of view", Inform = "Develop coherence and cohesion throughout text", Argue = "Support claims with reasons and evidence" [WIDA-LE].
- California Expanding: verbs "appropriate to the task, text type, and discipline for an increasing variety of familiar and new topics" (CDE summary of PII.3.Ex); "Expand noun phrases in a variety of ways (e.g., adding comparative/superlative adjectives to noun phrases or simple clause embedding)" [CA PII.6.4.Ex]; combine "compound and complex sentences ... to express a reason ... to make a concession" (grade 6 Bridging examples; the Expanding step is the intermediate rung, inference) [CA PII.6.6.Br]; cohesion via "pronouns or synonyms" [CA PII.6.2a.Ex]; ELPAC Level 2 = "low to midrange of the 'Expanding' proficiency level" [ELPAC-PLD].

Grammar to control
- **Complex sentences** "with subject-verb agreement and avoidance of splices, run-ons, and fragments" [TEKS 6.10.D.i, 7.10.D.i, 8.10.D.i]; "Use subordinating and coordinating conjunctions" [VA17 6.8]; "subordinating conjunctions to form complex sentences and correlative conjunctions", conjunctive adverbs [TEKS 7.10.D.vi, iii].
- Tense system: "consistent, appropriate use of verb tenses" [TEKS 7.10.D.ii]; "Maintain consistent verb tense across paragraphs" [VA17 6.8, 8.8; VA24 8.LU.1.E]; present perfect vs past simple, present perfect continuous, past perfect, future continuous, *will/going to* for prediction, past-tense responses [CI-B1]; CA Bridging examples include "the present perfect to describe previously made claims or conclusions" [CA PII.6.3.Br] — target the perfect here, control it at Band 4 (inference).
- Modals: *must/have to*; *might, may, will, probably*; *must/can't* for deduction; *should have/might have* [CI-B1].
- Conditionals: second and third [CI-B1].
- Passive: "simple passive" [CI-B1]; recognise "active and passive voice" [TEKS 8.10.D.ii] — introduce, do not require productive control (inference; see D6).
- Reported speech across a range of tenses; wh-questions in the past; complex question tags [CI-B1].
- Agreement: "subject-verb agreement with intervening phrases and clauses" [VA17 6.8, 7.8.c]; "prepositions and prepositional phrases and their influence on subject-verb agreement" [TEKS 7.10.D.iv, 8.10.D.iii]; "pronoun-antecedent agreement to include indefinite pronouns" [VA17 6.8, 7.8.b]; "indefinite, reflexive, and relative pronouns" [VA24 8.LU.1.B]; "pronouns in prepositional phrases with compound objects" [VA17 8.8].
- Adjectives/adverbs: adverbs that "describe verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs" [VA17 6.8]; "comparative and superlative degrees in adverbs and adjectives" [VA17 8.8]; "specific adjectives and adverbs to enhance" writing [VA24 8.LU.1.C]; broader intensifiers (*too, enough*) [CI-B1].
- Noun phrases with embedded relative clauses ("She's the doctor who saved the animals", grade 6 condensing example) [CA PII.6.7]; abstract nouns [WIDA-LC 6-8 L4].
- Adverbials: CA Bridging "adverbs, adverb phrases and clauses, prepositional phrases" for "time, manner, place, cause" [CA PII.6.5.Br]; "Arrange phrases and clauses within a sentence to improve meaning" [VA24 8.LU.1.D].
- Connecting words "expressing cause and effect, contrast etc." [CI-B1]; CA grade-5 Expanding example "for example, in the first place" [CA App-B].
- Mechanics: commas "to set off words, phrases, and clauses, and semicolons" [TEKS 7.10.D.viii]; "commas in nonrestrictive phrases and clauses, semicolons, colons, and parentheses" [TEKS 8.10.D.vi]; "avoiding comma splices and run-ons" [VA24 8.LU.2.A]; "quotation marks with dialogue and direct quotations" [VA17 7.8.e, 8.8; VA24 8.LU.2.B]; "correct spelling, including commonly confused terms" [TEKS 7.10.D.ix, 8.10.D.vii].

Writing expectations
- Multi-paragraph narrative, informative/explanatory and first argumentative pieces (claim + reasons + evidence) [WIDA-LE Narrate/Inform/Argue; WIDA-KLU]; "Revise writing for clarity of content, word choice, sentence variety, and transition among paragraphs" [VA24 8.W.3.A]; edit "for standard grammar and usage, including subject-verb agreement, pronoun agreement, and appropriate verb tenses" [TX ELPS (c)(5)(D)]; "a variety of grade-appropriate sentence lengths, patterns, and connecting words to combine phrases, clauses, and sentences" [TX ELPS (c)(5)(F)].
- Short, simple essay [CEFR-RE B1]; paragraph-level cohesion with "a variety of connecting words" and pronoun/synonym reference chains [CA App-B; CA PII.6.2a.Ex].
- Research/citation: "display academic citations and use source materials ethically" [TEKS 7.12.I, 8.12.I]; "Cite primary and secondary sources using ... (MLA) or ... (APA) style" [VA17 8.9; VA24 8.R.1.F]; "Cite primary and secondary sources" and "Avoid plagiarism by using own words" [VA17 6.9]. Treat MLA/APA as *introduced* (in-text citation + simple works-cited list), mastered at Band 4 (inference; see D8).

Vocabulary
- "a good range of vocabulary related to familiar topics and everyday situations" but "major errors still occur when expressing more complex thoughts" [CEFR-VR B1; CEFR vocabulary control B1]; "Words and expressions with expressive meaning through use of collocations and idioms" [WIDA-PD L4]; "emerging grade-appropriate vocabulary" [TX ELPS (d)(6)(C)(iii)(II)].

### Band 4 — CEFR B2 (WIDA 4-5 · CA Bridging · TX Advanced High · VA grades 9-10)

Writer profile
- "Can write clear, detailed texts on a variety of subjects ... synthesising and evaluating information and arguments from a number of sources" [CEFR-WP B2]; "an essay or report that develops an argument systematically with appropriate highlighting of significant points and relevant supporting detail" [CEFR-RE B2]; "Good grammatical control; occasional 'slips' or non-systematic errors ... are rare" [CEFR-GA B2]; "a limited number of cohesive devices ... though there may be some 'jumpiness' in a long contribution" [CEFR-CO B2].
- Texas Advanced High: writing "nearly comparable to writing of native English-speaking peers in clarity and precision"; "occasional difficulty with naturalness of phrasing and expression"; errors "minor and usually limited to low-frequency words and structures; errors rarely interfere with communication" [TX ELPS (d)(6)(D)(iii)]. Revised ELPS Advanced: "write a variety of sentence types using standard convention"; "past, present, perfect, progressive, or future tense verbs with subject-verb agreement with accuracy"; "precise content-area language to narrate, describe, explain, or justify" [TX ELPS-26 L5].
- WIDA: "Multiple, complex sentences" / "A variety of complex grammatical structures matched to purpose" (L5) [WIDA-PD L5]; "compound and complex sentences, with deliberate clause variation", "modal verbs, hedging, and evaluative expressions", cohesion via "given/new information, whole/part relationships, and paragraph openers" (L5) [WIDA-LC 6-8 L5]; "substitution, ellipsis, and parallelism" (L4) [WIDA-LC 6-8 L4]. Argue: "Introduce and develop claim(s) and acknowledge counterclaim(s)", "Establish and maintain formal style" [WIDA-LE].
- California Bridging: "compound, complex, and compound–complex sentences ... to show the relationship between multiple events or ideas ... or to evaluate an argument" [CA PII.7.6.Br]; "Expand noun phrases in an increasing variety of ways (e.g., more complex clause embedding)" [CA PII.7.4.Br]; "Use various verb types (e.g., doing, saying, being/having, thinking/feeling, reporting), tenses (e.g., present, past, future, simple, progressive, perfect) appropriate to the task, text type, and discipline" [CA PII.6.3.Br]; cohesion through "pronouns, synonyms, or nominalizations" [CA PII.6.2a.Br]; ELPAC Level 3 = "upper range of 'Expanding' ... through the lower range of 'Bridging'" [ELPAC-PLD].

Grammar to control
- "a variety of complete, controlled sentences and avoidance of unintentional splices, run-ons, and fragments" [TEKS E1.9.D.i]; "Construct simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences" [VA24 8.LU.1.A]; "Craft and apply a variety of sentence structures" [VA24 9.LU.1.B].
- "parallel structure across sentences and paragraphs" [VA17 9.7; VA24 9.LU.1.A]; "appositives and main and subordinate clauses" [VA17 9.7; VA24 9.LU.1.D].
- Voice: "consistent, appropriate use of verb tense and active and passive voice" [TEKS E1.9.D.ii]; "Use and apply the active and passive voice as appropriate" [VA24 9.LU.1.C]; full range of passives, narrative tenses, past perfect (continuous), future perfect (continuous) [CI-B2].
- Conditionals: mixed; *wish*; *would* for past habits [CI-B2].
- Modals of deduction and speculation; *can't have, needn't have* [CI-B2]; hedging and evaluative expressions [WIDA-LC 6-8 L5].
- Relative clauses (defining/non-defining) and reported speech extended [CI-B2]; complex clause embedding in noun phrases [CA PII.7.4.Br].
- Agreement maintained across long subjects and inverted structures; "pronoun-antecedent agreement" retained as an editing focus [TEKS E1.9.D.iii].
- Adjectives and adverbs used for precision and register [CI-B2]; "Maintain consistent verb tense" [VA24 9.LU.1.E].
- Mechanics: "commas, semicolons, colons, and dashes to set off phrases and clauses" [TEKS E1.9.D.v]; English II adds parentheses [TEKS E2.9.D.v]; "commas and semicolons to distinguish and divide main and subordinate clauses" [VA17 9.7; VA24 9.LU.2.A]; colon usage [VA17 10.7]; "Spell correctly, consulting reference materials" [VA24 9.LU.2.C].

Writing expectations
- Persuasive/argumentative and analytic essays with thesis, counterclaims and textual evidence [VA17 10.6]; "Edit writing for appropriate conventions, style, and language" and "Revise writing for clarity of content, accuracy, and adequate elaboration" [VA24 9.W.3]; "narrate, describe, and explain with increasing specificity and detail" [TX ELPS (c)(5)(G)].
- Research product citing "sources for quoted and paraphrased ideas using a standard method of documentation, such as the Modern Language Association (MLA) or APA" [VA24 9.R.1.F-G; VA17 9.8, 10.8]; "Apply a style manual, such as that of the Modern Language Association (MLA) or APA" [VA24 9.LU.2.B]; "display academic citations, including for paraphrased and quoted text, and use source materials ethically to avoid plagiarism" [TEKS E1.11.H].

Vocabulary
- WIDA Bridging: "Technical and abstract content-area language, including content-specific collocations" [WIDA-PD L5]; "modal verbs, hedging, and evaluative expressions" [WIDA-LC 6-8 L5]; Texas: errors confined to "low-frequency words" [TX ELPS (d)(6)(D)(iii)]; CEFR B2 vocabulary descriptor not recovered verbatim — treat as "good range for own field, some circumlocution" (inference from CEFR-VR B1 and C1 endpoints).

### Band 5 — CEFR C1 (WIDA 5-6, grades 9-12 cluster · CA Bridging-exit/RFEP · beyond TX Advanced High · VA grades 11-12)

Writer profile
- "Can write clear, well-structured texts of complex subjects, underlining the relevant salient issues, expanding and supporting points of view at some length with subsidiary points, reasons and relevant examples, and rounding off with an appropriate conclusion" [CEFR-WP C1]; "Consistently maintains a high degree of grammatical accuracy; errors are rare and difficult to spot" [CEFR-GA C1]; "controlled use of organisational patterns, connectors and cohesive devices" [CEFR-CO C1]; "Layout, paragraphing and punctuation are consistent and helpful. Spelling is accurate, apart from occasional slips" [CEFR-OC C1].
- WIDA (grades 9-12 only, since MODEL reaches C1 only in this cluster [WIDA-CEFR]): "an increasingly complex variety of sentence types, showing relationships, including embedded clauses and parallel grammatical structures"; "strategic and precise language choices to refine arguments, strengthen persuasion, and eliminate ambiguity"; "well-developed, coherent, and cohesive ideas using varied organizational patterns with strong logical relationships and audience awareness" [WIDA-LC 9-12 L6]; Level 6 is "open-ended as language development continues" [WIDA 2020 / MinneTESOL].
- California (grades 11-12 Bridging, the ceiling of the ELD standards): "Condense ideas in a variety of ways (e.g., through a variety of embedded clauses, or by compounding verb or prepositional phrases, nominalization) to create precise simple, compound, and complex sentences that condense concrete and abstract ideas" [CA PII.11-12.7.Br]; nominalisation "collapses an entire clause or even multiple clauses into nouns or noun phrases" [CA App-B]; ELPAC Level 4 = "upper range of the 'Bridging' proficiency level" [ELPAC-PLD]. Beyond this the learner is reclassified and served by ELA standards (inference; see D3).
- Texas: no ELPS level above Advanced High; the 2026-27 "Advanced" ceiling is "precise content-area language to narrate, describe, explain, or justify" [TX ELPS-26 L5]. Band 5 therefore draws on TEKS English II-IV and Virginia 11-12 (inference).

Grammar to control
- "verbal phrases to achieve sentence conciseness and variety" [VA24 11.LU.1; VA17 11.7]; "complex sentence structure to infuse sentence variety" [VA24 11.LU.1; VA17 11.7, 12.7]; "Differentiate and apply active and passive voice to convey a desired effect" [VA24 11.LU.1].
- Nominalisation and clause embedding as condensing devices [CA PII.11-12.7.Br]; embedded clauses and parallel grammatical structures [WIDA-LC 9-12 L6].
- Inversion with negative adverbials; mixed conditionals in past, present and future; modals in the past; *wish/if only* regrets; all passive forms incl. narrative passives; phrasal verbs incl. splitting [CI-C1].
- Register control: differentiate "contexts that call for formal English and situations where informal discourse is more appropriate" [VA24 11.LU]; "Establish and maintain formal style" [WIDA-LE Argue].
- Mechanics: "commas, semi-colons, and colons correctly in complex sentences" [VA24 11.LU.2]; "Write and edit work so that it conforms to the guidelines in style manual, such as ... (MLA) or ... (APA)" [VA24 11.LU.2]; style-manual rules for punctuating and formatting direct quotations (VA 2017 grade 12) [VA17 12.7-12.8]; dashes, parentheses [TEKS E2.9.D.v].

Writing expectations
- Argumentation "for multiple purposes and audiences" written "to a standard acceptable both in the workplace and in post-secondary education" [VA17 12.6; VA24 11.W.3]; expositions "of complex subjects, underlining the relevant salient issues" [CEFR-RE C1].
- Full research product: "Cite primary and secondary sources for quoted and paraphrased ideas using a standard method of documentation such as ... (MLA) or ... (APA)" [VA24 11.R.1; VA17 11.8, 12.8]; source evaluation for credibility and bias, ethical use [VA17 10.8; TEKS E1.11.H].
- Discourse: "adapting organizational patterns to enhance clarity, logical flow, and audience engagement" [WIDA-LC 6-8 L6]; "academic connecting and transitional words" such as *consequently, specifically, however* (CA grade-5 Bridging example) [CA App-B].

Vocabulary
- "a good command of a broad lexical repertoire allowing gaps to be readily overcome with circumlocutions" [CEFR-VR C1]; "precise choices, adding shades of meaning and figurative language" [WIDA-LC 6-8 L6]; "a variety of words and phrases, including evaluation, obligation, idioms, and collocations" (grades 4-5 L6 wording) [WIDA 2020 PLD snapshot].

## D. Where the frameworks disagree, and what I chose

1. **Age-graded vs proficiency-graded standards.** TEKS and Virginia SOL are grade-level (age) standards; CA ELD, TX ELPS and WIDA are proficiency levels that apply at any age. I used the state ELA grade bands only to decide *which conventions and sentence structures* belong to a band, not topics or text difficulty; an adult Band 1 learner should meet grade-2 *conventions* through adult content (inference).
2. **Different level counts.** WIDA has 6 levels (Level 6 open-ended), CA has 3 (Emerging/Expanding/Bridging, with ELPAC reporting 4), Texas currently 4 (Beginning/Intermediate/Advanced/Advanced High) moving to 5 in 2026-27 (Pre-Production/Beginning/Intermediate/High Intermediate/Advanced). Level numbers are therefore not interchangeable; each band in Section A spans two adjacent WIDA levels, which is consistent with Pearson's overlapping WIDA→CEFR ranges and with WIDA's own caution that "connections should not be made between WIDA proficiency levels and CEFR levels" (only MODEL Online scale scores were linked).
3. **Where C1 sits.** All three ELD frameworks top out at "nearly comparable to native peers" (TX Advanced High; CA Bridging; WIDA 5-6), which for K-12 writers is closer to B2 than to the adult-academic C1. WIDA MODEL reaches C1 only in grades 9-12 (B2/C1 in 6-8; B2 in 3-5). I therefore anchored Band 5 to WIDA 5-6 *in the 9-12 cluster*, CA Bridging-exit / grades 11-12 Bridging descriptors, and mainstream grade 11-12 ELA (VA 11.LU, TEKS English II-IV); Band 4 = Advanced High / Bridging / WIDA 4-5.
4. **The new Texas ELPS relabels the scale.** "Advanced High" disappears; "High Intermediate" and "Advanced" split it, and "Pre-Production" is added below Beginning. Reading the 2026-27 writing PLDs (phrases at Intermediate; sentences with transition words at High Intermediate; "a variety of sentence types" at Advanced), the new labels sit roughly one step *lower* than the old ones: old Intermediate ≈ new High Intermediate wording. I list both scales in Section A; use the current §74.4 scale until 2026-27, then the mapping Band 1 = Pre-Production/Beginning, Band 2 = Intermediate, Band 3 = High Intermediate, Band 4 = Advanced (inference).
5. **Tense ordering.** TEKS grade 2-3 already require "past, present, and future verb tense" and Virginia grade 2 requires irregular past verbs, whereas the Core Inventory places past simple at A1 but present perfect at A2 and past perfect at B1, and CA ELD adds the perfect only at Expanding/Bridging. Texas ELPS confirms that L2 writers at Beginning/Intermediate actually control present tense first. I followed the ELD/CEFR ordering (present → past → future → perfect) and treated the TEKS grade-2 list as a ceiling reached by the *end* of Band 2, not the start of Band 1.
6. **Passive voice.** CI puts simple passive at B1 and full passives at B2/C1; TEKS grade 8 and VA grade 9 name active/passive; TEKS English I requires "consistent, appropriate use of ... active and passive voice". Chosen: recognise and use the simple passive at Band 3, control voice choice at Band 4, exploit passives stylistically at Band 5.
7. **Compound-complex sentences.** CA grade 7 Bridging and VA 2024 grade 8 name them; CEFR/CI never name sentence types but B2 is where "narrative tenses", "relative clauses" and full cohesion arrive. Chosen: Band 4 (with a Band 3 preview through complex sentences).
8. **When MLA/APA starts.** Virginia names MLA/APA at grade 8 (2017 8.9; 2024 8.R.1.F); TEKS grade 3 asks for a "works cited page" and grades 7-8 for "academic citations" without naming a style; English I adds citations "for paraphrased and quoted text". CEFR is silent on citation. Chosen: works-cited list at Band 2, MLA/APA in-text + list introduced at Band 3, applied throughout a research essay at Band 4, full style-manual conformity (formatting, quotation punctuation) at Band 5.
9. **WIDA descriptor sources.** The 2020 PLD tables (Appendix D of the framework) could not be extracted verbatim; I used WIDA's own Language Charts (aligned to the 2020 PLDs) and the 2012 Performance Definitions, both official WIDA documents. Note that the Language Charts describe the same behaviour one level lower in the 9-12 cluster than in 6-8 (e.g., 6-8 Level 2 wording = 9-12 Level 1 wording), which is why Band 5 is anchored to the 9-12 cluster.
10. **California's three levels vs ELPAC's four.** ELPAC Level 2 corresponds to "low to midrange of Expanding" and Level 3 to "upper Expanding through lower Bridging", so CA levels are wide; I split Emerging across Bands 1-2 and Bridging across Bands 4-5 (inference supported by the PII.6 clause-combining ladder quoted in A).
11. **Virginia numbering changed in 2024.** The 2017 SOL (e.g., 5.8, 8.8, 9.7, 11.7) are still referenced in many district documents; the 2024 SOL reorganise the same content into Language Usage codes (g.LU.1 Grammar, g.LU.2 Mechanics), Writing (g.W.3 Usage and Mechanics) and Research (g.R.1). Both are listed. Substantive 2024 changes relevant here: compound-complex sentences named at grade 8 (8.LU.1.A) and "Apply a style manual" moved into Language Usage at grade 9 (9.LU.2.B).
12. **ACTFL cross-check.** ACTFL's official one-directional correspondence (OPI/OPIc/WPT → CEFR) is Novice High = A1, Intermediate Low = A2, Intermediate Mid/High = B1.1/B1.2, Advanced Low/Mid = B2.1/B2.2, Advanced High = C1, Superior = C2. It is consistent with the bands and was used only as a sanity check.
13. **No official crosswalks exist** between CEFR and CA ELD or TX ELPS/TELPAS; the only official CEFR link for WIDA is the MODEL Online scale-score concordance (2023 study, published Jan 2024), plus Pearson's GSE alignment for grades 6-8. Everything in Section A beyond those two documents is inference from descriptor content, flagged as such.
