# Sample lesson, written form — 4F-B3 "Just, already and yet" (Present Perfect, Building)

This is the written version of the lesson that the animated story tells. Every lesson has both:
the story video (60-90 seconds) and this page. They say the same thing in the same order, so a
student can watch, read, or do both. Language level: Building (A2). Sentences are short. One idea
per line. No grammar words before the example.

---

## One morning with Ana

**8:06.** Ana runs to the bus stop. The bus left at 8:05.

> **The bus has just left.**

*just* = a short time ago.

**9:00.** At work. Her manager asks, "Can you send the report?" Ana sent it at 8:40.

> **I have already sent it.**

*already* = earlier than expected.

**12:00.** A friend asks, "Have you eaten yet?" Ana has not eaten.

> **No, I haven't eaten yet.**

*yet* = until now.

---

## Where the word goes

| Word | Place | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **just** | between *have / has* and the main verb | I **have just** eaten. |
| **already** | between *have / has* and the main verb | She **has already** left. |
| **yet** | at the end, in questions and negatives | Have you finished **yet**? I haven't finished **yet**. |

The main verb is the third form: eaten, sent, left, done, finished.

## Watch out

| Not this | This |
|---|---|
| I just have eaten. | I **have just** eaten. |
| Have you yet eaten? | Have you eaten **yet**? |
| I already have finished. | I **have already** finished. |
| I haven't seen him already. | I haven't seen him **yet**. |

## Three sentences to say aloud

1. I have just finished work.
2. They have already gone home.
3. Has the shop opened yet?

## Now try it

Eight quick questions. If you miss one, the app shows you this page again at the right line.
