# Verbs and Tenses strand (replaces Section 4 "Verb tense & aspect")

This strand gives every tense and every verb form its own unit, its own exercises, its own
test and its own certificate. It follows the rules in `00-platform-frame.md`: explain one rule
per screen in plain words with examples first, 8-20 exercises per lesson from the exercise
catalogue, a 15-question test drawn at random from the unit's bank at that band (pass 12/15),
a certificate naming the unit and band, and remediation back to the exact lesson that teaches
each missed item.

Unit codes are 4A-4O. Where a code appears at several bands, the unit spirals: the same tense
is re-taught with new uses and harder contrasts. Small units are merged at the higher bands
(for example 4F+4G at Expanding) so that no certificate covers fewer than three lessons.

Lesson tags (for example `4B-F3`) are the remediation keys every bank item carries.

## How every tense unit is built (the fixed lesson pattern)

Every tense unit teaches the same five things in the same order, so a slow student always
knows what comes next:

1. **Form** — the pattern in a box (subject + verb form), positive, negative, question, short
   answer, contractions; then the spelling rule for that form.
2. **Meaning** — the one to three uses, each with a picture-in-words example from adult life
   (work, family, health, money, travel).
3. **Time words** — the signal words that go with the tense (yesterday, since, by next year).
4. **Contrast** — the one tense students confuse it with, side by side.
5. **Mistakes** — the five most common errors, by first language, shown wrong → right.

Exercise mix per lesson (8-20 items): `mc` (pick the form) · `gap` (type the verb) · `transform`
(change tense, make negative, make a question) · `error-spot` (tap the wrong verb) · `order`
(build the sentence) · `classify` (sort by tense or by use) · `match` (time word ↔ tense) ·
`next` (which word comes next). Every item is tagged with its lesson.

Bank composition per unit × band (100-120 items): 35% form, 35% meaning-in-context, 20%
contrast with the confusable tense, 10% error correction. Contexts are adult and general
knowledge; no children's-classroom topics.

## 4A — Be, have and do (Foundations only)

Purpose: the three verbs every other tense is built from. Nothing else in the strand can be
taught until these are automatic.

| Tag | Lesson | Rule in plain words | Examples | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4A-F1 | Be in the present: am / is / are | I → am; he, she, it, one person or thing → is; you, we, they, more than one → are | I am a nurse. She is at work. They are tired. | Arabic, Russian, Chinese speakers drop "be": *She tired.* |
| 4A-F2 | Be in the past: was / were | I, he, she, it → was; you, we, they → were | I was late. We were at home. | *You was* (accepted in speech in some dialects; wrong in the test) |
| 4A-F3 | Negatives and contractions of be | be + not; I'm / you're / he's / isn't / aren't / wasn't / weren't | He isn't here. We weren't ready. | *He no is here.* (Spanish, Portuguese) |
| 4A-F4 | Questions and short answers with be | Put be before the subject; answer with the pronoun + be | Is she a doctor? — Yes, she is. / No, she isn't. | *She is a doctor?* is fine in speech; the written form moves "is" first |
| 4A-F5 | Have and has (and have got) | I, you, we, they → have; he, she, it → has; "have got" = have (informal, British) | I have two jobs. She has a car. We've got time. | *She have*; *I am having a car* (no continuous for possession) |
| 4A-F6 | Do and does as helpers | For other verbs, questions and negatives use do / does (present) and did (past); the main verb stays in its base form | Do you work here? She doesn't drive. Did they call? | *Does she works?* *He didn't went.* |

Also taught inside 4A: the idea of a helping verb (auxiliary) versus a main verb; be as a main
verb (She is tired) versus be as a helper (She is working). Transitive/intransitive is not
named here; it is taught in 4L.

Bank: 120 items. Test: 15. Certificate: **Be, Have and Do, Foundations level**.

## 4B — Present simple (Foundations → Building → Connecting)

| Tag | Lesson | Rule in plain words | Examples | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4B-F1 | Form: base verb; he / she / it + -s | Add -s for one person or thing that is not I or you | I work. He works. The shop opens at nine. | The missing -s is the most common error at every level: *She work.* |
| 4B-F2 | Spelling of the -s form | -s; -es after s, sh, ch, x, o (watches, goes); consonant + y → -ies (studies); have → has | fixes, washes, tries, does | *studys*, *gos* |
| 4B-F3 | Negatives: don't / doesn't + base verb | The -s moves to "does"; the main verb loses it | She doesn't work on Sundays. | *She doesn't works.* |
| 4B-F4 | Questions: Do / Does + subject + base verb; short answers | Question word first: Where does she work? | Do you like tea? — Yes, I do. | *Where she works?* |
| 4B-F5 | Meaning 1: habits and routines; frequency words | Things you do again and again; always / usually / often / sometimes / never go before the verb, after be | I usually walk to work. He is never late. | *I go always* (Spanish, French) |
| 4B-F6 | Meaning 2: facts and general truths; time words | Things that are always true; every day, on Mondays, twice a week | Water boils at 100 degrees. The bank closes at five. | Using continuous for facts: *The sun is rising in the east.* |
| 4B-B1 | Timetables and schedules (present simple for the future) | Fixed public schedules use the present simple even for future time | The train leaves at 6:40 tomorrow. The course starts in May. | *The train will leave at 6:40* is possible but not the schedule form |
| 4B-B2 | Stative verbs (verbs of thinking, feeling, having, being) | know, believe, want, need, like, love, hate, own, belong, seem, cost, mean: normally not in the continuous | I want a coffee. She knows the answer. This costs ten dollars. | *I am wanting*, *She is knowing* (very common for Hindi, Punjabi, Arabic speakers) |
| 4B-B3 | Present simple vs present continuous | Simple = always / usually; continuous = now / these days | I work in a bank. This week I'm working from home. | Mixing them in one sentence with the wrong signal word |
| 4B-B4 | Instructions, directions and commentaries | Present simple for steps and directions: You turn left and take the second road | First you boil the water, then you add the tea. | Imperative vs present simple: both correct, different feel |
| 4B-C1 | Present simple in time clauses about the future | After when, as soon as, until, before, after, once: present simple, not will | Call me when you arrive. I'll wait until she comes. | *when you will arrive* (very common in every first language) |
| 4B-C2 | Narrative and headline present | Telling a story or a joke in the present; news headlines drop be | So he walks in and says … / "Minister resigns" | Register: fine in speech and headlines, not in formal reports |
| 4B-C3 | Present simple in zero conditionals and general rules | If + present, present: for things that are always true | If you heat ice, it melts. | Link to Section 6 (conditionals) |

Banks: Foundations 120, Building 110, Connecting (merged with 4C as "Present tenses in use") 110.
Certificates: **Present Simple, Foundations level**; **Present Simple, Building level**; at
Connecting, **Present Tenses in Use, Connecting level** (4B+4C).

## 4C — Present continuous (Foundations → Building → Connecting)

| Tag | Lesson | Rule in plain words | Examples | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4C-F1 | Form: am / is / are + verb-ing | Two parts: be + -ing. Both are needed. | I am working. They are waiting. | Dropping be: *I working.* Dropping -ing: *I am work.* |
| 4C-F2 | Spelling of -ing | drop silent e (make → making); double the last consonant after one short vowel (run → running, stop → stopping); ie → ying (lie → lying) | writing, sitting, beginning, lying | *writeing*, *runing* |
| 4C-F3 | Meaning 1: right now | Actions happening at the moment of speaking; now, at the moment, look!, listen! | She is talking on the phone right now. | Present simple for now: *She talks on the phone now.* |
| 4C-F4 | Negatives, questions and short answers | be + not + -ing; be before the subject in questions | Are you listening? — Yes, I am. He isn't sleeping. | *Are you listen?* |
| 4C-F5 | Present continuous vs present simple (first contrast) | now vs usually | I usually drink tea, but today I'm drinking coffee. | |
| 4C-B1 | Meaning 2: around now and temporary situations | Things in progress these days, this month, even if not at this second | I'm reading a good book. She's staying with her sister this month. | |
| 4C-B2 | Meaning 3: future arrangements | Plans already fixed with other people, usually with a time | We're meeting the doctor at ten tomorrow. | Contrast with going to (4I-B3) |
| 4C-B3 | Meaning 4: changes and trends | Things that are getting more or less; with more and more, getting | Prices are rising. It's getting colder. | |
| 4C-B4 | Always + continuous for annoyance | He's always losing his keys = it happens too often (a complaint) | She's always interrupting me. | Not the same as "He always loses his keys" (a plain fact) |
| 4C-C1 | Stative verbs that change meaning in the continuous | think (opinion) vs be thinking (process); have (own) vs be having (experience); see (understand) vs be seeing (meet) | I think it's good. / I'm thinking about the offer. We're having lunch. | |
| 4C-C2 | Softening with the continuous | I'm hoping, I was wondering, I'm looking forward to: polite, less direct | I'm hoping you can help. | Register lesson; links to Section 12 |

Banks: Foundations 110, Building 110. Certificates: **Present Continuous, Foundations level**;
**Present Continuous, Building level**; Connecting shared with 4B.

## 4D — Past simple (Foundations → Building → Connecting)

| Tag | Lesson | Rule in plain words | Examples | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4D-F1 | Was / were (review) and the idea of finished time | The past is finished: yesterday, last week, in 2010, ago | I was in Turkey last year. | |
| 4D-F2 | Regular past: verb + -ed; spelling | -ed; -d after e (lived); double consonant after one short vowel (stopped); consonant + y → -ied (studied) | worked, lived, planned, carried | *stoped*, *studyed* |
| 4D-F3 | Irregular past forms, first set | Some verbs change completely; learn them in groups (see 4N) | go → went, have → had, see → saw, make → made | *goed*, *maked* |
| 4D-F4 | Negatives and questions with did | did + base verb; the main verb goes back to its base form | I didn't go. Did you see it? Where did they live? | *I didn't went*, *Did you saw?* |
| 4D-F5 | Time words and ago | yesterday, last night, two days ago, in 1999, when I was a child | We moved here ten years ago. | *before ten years* (Arabic, Persian, Turkish) |
| 4D-F6 | Telling what happened in order | then, after that, next, finally with past simple; short life stories | I got up, had breakfast and left. | Overusing "and then" — teach variety |
| 4D-B1 | Irregular past forms, second set (with 4N) | | buy → bought, teach → taught, bring → brought | Past form vs past participle confusion begins here |
| 4D-B2 | Pronunciation and reading of -ed | -ed reads as /t/ (worked), /d/ (lived) or /ɪd/ (wanted, needed); a reading note, no audio needed | | Adding a syllable to every -ed |
| 4D-B3 | Past simple for past habits and states | Repeated actions in the past with every day, always; states with was, had, lived | When I lived in Cairo I worked nights. | Link to used to (4J) |
| 4D-B4 | Past simple vs present perfect (first look) | Finished time → past simple; time up to now → present perfect | I saw her yesterday. I've seen her today. | Full treatment in 4F-C1 |
| 4D-C1 | Narrative past: past simple as the main story line | The events that move a story forward are in the past simple; description around them uses other tenses | The phone rang. She picked it up. | |
| 4D-C2 | Past simple with when-clauses | When + past simple, past simple: one action after another | When the alarm went off, I got up. | Contrast with "was getting up" (4E) |

## 4F — Present perfect (Building → Connecting → Expanding)

The most important and most confused tense for ESL learners. It gets the most lessons.

| Tag | Lesson | Rule in plain words | Examples | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4F-B1 | Form: have / has + past participle | Past participle = third form (gone, seen, worked) | I have finished. She has left. | Using the past form: *I have saw*; dropping have: *I finished already* is fine in American English, but teach the full form |
| 4F-B2 | Life experience with ever and never | Any time up to now, no date given | Have you ever been to Japan? I've never eaten sushi. | Adding a finished time: *I have been there in 2015* |
| 4F-B3 | Just, already and yet | just = a short time ago; already = earlier than expected; yet = until now (questions and negatives, at the end) | I've just eaten. She's already left. Have you finished yet? | Position of yet |
| 4F-B4 | For and since | for + a length of time; since + a starting point | I've lived here for six years / since 2020. | *since six years* (very common) |
| 4F-B5 | Gone vs been | has gone = is still there; has been = went and came back | He's gone to the bank (he's there). He's been to the bank (he's back). | |
| 4F-C1 | Present perfect vs past simple (the full contrast) | Finished time word → past simple; time up to now or no time → present perfect | I lost my keys yesterday. / I've lost my keys (they're still lost). | The central test item type at Connecting |
| 4F-C2 | Unfinished time periods | today, this week, this year, so far, recently, lately | I've had three meetings today (the day isn't over). | |
| 4F-C3 | News, results and changes | Present result of a past action; announcing news | The price has gone up. Our team has won! | |
| 4F-C4 | With superlatives and "the first time" | It's the best film I've ever seen. This is the first time I've driven a truck. | | *the best film I ever saw* is common American usage; accepted as a variant |
| 4F-C5 | Questions: How long…? How many…? How often…? | How long have you worked here? How many times have you moved? | | |
| 4F-E1 | Present perfect in reports and academic writing | Summarising what research has shown; "Studies have found …" | Several studies have reported … | Link to the writing strand |
| 4F-E2 | British and American differences | Americans often use the past simple with just / already / yet; both accepted in answer keys | Did you eat yet? / Have you eaten yet? | Convention taught: American classroom usage; variant accepted |
| 4F-E3 | Present perfect in time clauses about the future | when / after / once + present perfect for completion | Call me when you have finished. | |

Banks: Building 110, Connecting 110; Expanding merged with 4G (110). Certificates: **Present
Perfect, Building level**; **Present Perfect, Connecting level**; **Present Perfect Simple and
Continuous, Expanding level** (4F+4G).

## 4I — Future forms (Building → Connecting → Expanding → Mastery)

English has no single future tense; students must learn which form fits which situation.

| Tag | Lesson | Rule in plain words | Examples | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4I-B1 | Will: decisions made now, offers, promises, requests | will + base verb; won't | I'll help you. I'll call you tonight. Will you open the window? | *I will to help*, *I will helping* |
| 4I-B2 | Will: predictions and beliefs | I think / probably / I'm sure + will | It will rain tomorrow. She'll pass the exam. | |
| 4I-B3 | Going to: plans and intentions | be going to + base verb for something already decided | I'm going to look for a new job. | *I going to*, *I'm going to looking* |
| 4I-B4 | Going to: predictions from evidence | Something you can see coming | Look at those clouds — it's going to rain. | |
| 4I-B5 | Present continuous for arrangements | Fixed with other people, usually with a time or place | We're flying to Dubai on Friday. | |
| 4I-C1 | Present simple for timetables (review) and future time clauses | schedules; when / after / until + present | The exam starts at nine. I'll leave when it finishes. | *when it will finish* |
| 4I-C2 | Choosing: will vs going to vs present continuous | decision now vs plan vs arrangement | | The core test item at Connecting |
| 4I-C3 | Future continuous | will be + -ing: in progress at a future time; also for neutral "what's planned" | This time next week I'll be lying on a beach. Will you be using the car? | |
| 4I-C4 | Shall, let's, how about, why don't we | Offers and suggestions | Shall I open it? Let's start. | Shall is rare in American English; accepted |
| 4I-C5 | Future with may, might, could (uncertainty) | Weaker than will | It might snow. We may be late. | Link to Section 6 |
| 4I-E1 | Future perfect | will have + past participle: finished before a future point; by + time | By June I'll have finished the course. | |
| 4I-E2 | Be about to, be due to, be to | Very near future; scheduled; formal instruction | The train is about to leave. The report is due to be published. You are to report at 8. | |
| 4I-E3 | Future in the past | was going to, would, was about to: a future seen from a past moment | I was going to call you, but I forgot. | |
| 4I-E4 | Future continuous for polite enquiries and predictions about routines | Will you be needing anything else? | | |
| 4I-M1 | Future perfect continuous | will have been + -ing: duration up to a future point | By 2030 she will have been teaching for forty years. | |
| 4I-M2 | Future forms in academic and business writing | Hedged futures (is expected to, is likely to, is projected to); avoiding will in reports | Sales are projected to rise. | Link to the writing strand |

Banks: Building 110, Connecting 110, Expanding 110; Mastery merged with 4O (110).
Certificates: **Future Forms, Building level**; **Future Forms, Connecting level**; **Future
Forms, Expanding level**; **Tense and Time in Formal Writing, Mastery level** (4I+4O).

## 4K — Passive voice (Building → Connecting → Expanding → Mastery)

| Tag | Lesson | Rule in plain words | Examples | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4K-B1 | Present simple passive: am / is / are + past participle | The thing that receives the action becomes the subject | Rice is grown in Egypt. These cars are made in Korea. | *is grow*, *is made by Korea* |
| 4K-B2 | Past simple passive: was / were + past participle | | The bridge was built in 1932. | Missing be: *The bridge built in 1932.* |
| 4K-B3 | By + agent, and when to leave the agent out | Leave it out when it is unknown, obvious or unimportant | My bag was stolen. (by someone — unknown) | Adding a useless agent: *by people* |
| 4K-B4 | When to choose the passive | The action or the receiver matters more than the doer; processes, news, notices | The office is cleaned every night. | Overuse in everyday speech |
| 4K-C1 | Passive with the perfect and continuous forms | has been + pp; is being + pp; was being + pp | The road has been closed. The car is being repaired. | *is been*, *has being* |
| 4K-C2 | Passive with modals | modal + be + past participle | It must be finished today. This can be done online. | *must finished* |
| 4K-C3 | Verbs with two objects | Either object can become the subject; the person is more common | She was given a prize. / A prize was given to her. | |
| 4K-C4 | Passive questions and negatives | Was it delivered? It wasn't delivered. Where was it made? | | |
| 4K-C5 | It is said that… / He is said to… (impersonal passive) | Reporting general opinion formally | It is believed that the site is 5,000 years old. He is thought to be in Spain. | |
| 4K-E1 | Passive infinitives and gerunds | to be done; being done; deserves to be seen; hates being told | The work needs to be checked. | |
| 4K-E2 | Get-passive | get + past participle in informal English, often for accidents | He got hurt. The window got broken. | Register: fine in speech, avoid in formal writing |
| 4K-E3 | Passive in reports and academic writing | Methods sections, formal notices; the sample was heated | | Link to writing strand |
| 4K-E4 | Avoiding the passive when it hides the doer | Active is often clearer; agentless passives in official writing | Mistakes were made → We made mistakes. | Link to Section 12 (style) |
| 4K-M1 | Passive with reporting verbs in complex forms | is believed to have + pp; is expected to be | The ship is believed to have sunk in 1850. | |
| 4K-M2 | Passive and active choices across a long text | Consistency, topic focus, old-before-new information | | Link to cohesion (Section 12) |

Banks: Building 110, Connecting 110, Expanding 110; Mastery merged with 4L+4M (110).
Certificates: **Passive Voice, Building level**; **Passive Voice, Connecting level**; **Passive
Voice, Expanding level**; **Verb Forms in Formal Writing, Mastery level** (4K+4L+4M).

## 4L — Verb patterns: gerund, infinitive, causatives (Connecting → Expanding → Mastery)

| Tag | Lesson | Rule in plain words | Examples | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4L-C1 | Verb + -ing | enjoy, avoid, finish, keep, mind, suggest, practise, consider, miss, give up | I enjoy cooking. Avoid driving at night. | *enjoy to cook* |
| 4L-C2 | Verb + to + base verb | want, need, decide, hope, plan, learn, promise, refuse, agree, manage, afford, offer | She decided to leave. | *decided leaving* |
| 4L-C3 | Verbs that take both with no change of meaning | start, begin, continue, like, love, hate, prefer (small differences taught later) | It started raining / to rain. | |
| 4L-C4 | Verbs that take both with a change of meaning | remember, forget, stop, try, regret, go on, mean | I stopped smoking (quit) / I stopped to smoke (paused in order to smoke). | The most tested contrast in the unit |
| 4L-C5 | Verb + object + to | ask, tell, want, allow, advise, remind, warn, expect someone to do | She asked me to wait. | *She said me to wait* (say vs tell, Section 10) |
| 4L-C6 | Bare infinitive after make, let, help; transitive and intransitive verbs named | make / let + object + base verb; help + (to) base verb; verbs that need an object (transitive) vs those that don't | They made us wait. Let me go. (arrive is intransitive: *arrive the airport*) | *made us to wait* |
| 4L-E1 | Preposition + -ing | interested in learning, good at, before leaving, without asking, look forward to seeing | | *look forward to see* |

## Remediation map

Every bank item carries one lesson tag (for example `4F-C1`). A failed test lists the missed
tags, opens those lessons again with a fresh exercise set, then offers a new test. Items that
test a contrast (present perfect vs past simple) carry the tag of the lesson that teaches the
contrast, not the tag of either tense alone.
